# Three small homes in the pocket: plans, precedents, rules and arrangements

Researched 27 September 2026 for the revised brief. Three single-level homes in the ~300 m² buildable pocket at 77 Foley Road, Woombye (Low Density Residential zone):
- a couple's two-bedroom main home, about 50% of the total floor area;
- two siblings' small homes, about 25% each.

The pocket is about 20 m wide at the road, narrowing to about 13 m at the back, and about 15 m deep. The land falls 3–4 m away from the road, so the buildings are high-set. The homes are fixed, not on wheels. The 50/25/25 split is a guide; using the pocket efficiently comes first.

This file builds on [planning-rules.md](planning-rules.md), [build-costs.md](build-costs.md) and [design-precedents.md](design-precedents.md) and does not repeat them. Every product, precedent and rule links to its source; all were accessed on 27 September 2026. These are notes, not architectural, legal or certification advice.

## Bottom line

- **Three homes of about 120–155 m² in total fit the pocket** in at least nine ways (section 5). Every footprint was checked against a conservative 248 m² version of the pocket.
  - The most efficient arrangements put the **siblings' homes at the road edge on short posts** and the **couple's home behind**, high-set, with parking under and the sun, deck and view to the NNE.
  - The two cheapest are **A3** (siblings' units attached to each other, with a breezeway to the couple's house) and **A8** (a single 16.5 × 7.5 m terrace of three along the road edge).
- **The legal framing matters more than the layout.** Three fully self-contained homes are a "multiple dwelling": an inconsistent use in this zone, needing impact assessment. "Dual occupancy + granny flat" is impossible by definition. Four framings work (section 4.2):
  - **F1:** one dwelling house with three wings sharing one laundry. No size cap, no fire walls. It relies on Council accepting kitchenettes without laundries.
  - **F2:** the couple's house plus sibling A's wing, with sibling B in a secondary dwelling of 60 m² or less.
  - **F3:** the couple's house plus **one** secondary dwelling holding both siblings with a shared laundry. The 60 m² cap means about **2 × 30 m²**, which gives exactly 50/25/25 at 120 m².
  - **F4:** a dual occupancy (code-assessable DA, charges, fails the slope benchmark).
- **Shared space is also legal evidence.** A shared court or deck, a shared laundry, one entry and no dividing fences support the "used in conjunction with" test. Fences between yards helped sink the houses in *Machado v Gold Coast* [2024] QPEC 22.
- **Sibling homes of about 30 m² are a proven size.** Queensland factory modules at 3.3–3.6 × 8–9 m are published:
  - Eastcoast Sunset 29.8 m², $85k ex GST;
  - Saltair Cardrona 29.6 m²;
  - Paradise Solaris-10 34 m², $135k inc GST.

  At 30 m² a unit holds an enclosed queen bedroom, a full kitchen run and a compliant bathroom. Livable-housing rules apply even to tiny homes in Queensland: 820 mm doors, 1000 mm corridors, and a bathroom of at least 1.8 × 2.2 m.
- **The couple's home fits best at 12 × 6 m (72 m²) or 10–13 × 6–7 m (60–80 m²).** Efficient two-bedroom depths are 6–7.3 m:
  - Frame Steel Seascape 72 (12 × 6);
  - Stroud Ashley 75 (11.17 × 6.82);
  - Imagine Casa (15.2 × 6.3).

  Kit shells cost $70k–$110k before labour and fit-out.
- **High-set is the gap in every product.** Only Saltair and The Shed House (both Sunshine Coast) say outright that they build on posts on slopes. No supplier prices 3–4 m posts, so budget them as site work per [build-costs.md](build-costs.md).

**What the small-home plans show.**
- **Width.** Queensland factory modules are **3.3–3.6 m wide** (Eastcoast 3.3, Paradise 3.4, Arcopod Nest 3.5, Shed House and Backyard Pods 3.6), which stays under the 3.5 m pilot-vehicle threshold or close to it ([design-precedents.md](design-precedents.md)). Some are 4.0–4.5 m (Designer Eco, Arcopod Stradbroke and Waco).
  - A **27–30 m² studio or one-bed is typically 3.3–3.6 × 8–9 m**.
  - A **35–45 m² one-bed is 3.5–4.5 × 10–12 m**.
- **Square pods.** Verified square pods are only Ecoshelta's 4.8 × 4.8 and 3.6 × 3.6 with extensions. No Queensland factory was found selling a 5 × 5 or 6 × 6 Class 1a single module, because 5–6 m exceeds normal transport width. A 6 × 6 pod would be two 3 × 6 modules or a site-built kit (Wide Span, The Shed House).
- **High-set.** Two Sunshine Coast suppliers state outright that they build on slopes on posts or stumps: **Saltair** (stump-and-rail) and **The Shed House** (steel suspended floors). Eastcoast, Paradise, Backyard Pods and Granny Flats 4U install on stumps or piers. **None publishes a price or maximum height for 3–4 m posts.** Price those as site work (engineering, bracing, stairs, balustrades, crane).
- **Class 1a.** Paradise and Granny Flats 4U state Class 1a. Eastcoast sells "park cabins", so confirm with them that the product is certified as a Class 1a dwelling rather than a caravan-park cabin.
- **Price.** Turnkey one-beds of 30–40 m² land at about **$82k–$103k ex GST (Eastcoast, excluding delivery and footings)** to **$135k–$180k (Paradise, Designer Eco, Superior)**, before site costs.

**What the 2-bed plans show.**
- **Efficient depths.** Stick-built and kit two-bedroom homes sit at **6.0–7.3 m deep** (Seascape 6, Casa 6.3, Cabin 6.7, Ashley 6.82, Retreat 7.26). That matches the passive-design rule of 6–7 m for through-ventilation in [design-precedents.md](design-precedents.md).
- **Single modules** are 4.0–4.8 m wide and 12–18 m long. They are too long for this pocket's depth unless laid across it; the pocket is 13–20 m wide.
- **60 m² is a natural product size.** Nine Frame Steel designs, the Everyday Lodge, Able Baringa and Wide Span Cabin 60 all sit at 60 m². A second bathroom or a separate laundry appears at **72–80 m²**.
- **Combined bath/laundry** (Designer Eco, Stroud Mini-Tego option) or a **European laundry cupboard** (Stroud Ashley) is how 56–75 m² plans fit two bedrooms.
- **For this pocket the best-fitting couple's homes are 12 × 6 m (72 m²) and 10–11 × 6–7 m (60–77 m²)**: Seascape 72, Seascape 60, Ashley 75, Retreat 80 (13.8 m, just fits at the back where the pocket is 13 m, so it needs checking).

**Patterns worth copying:**
1. **Nesting.** Each home's glazed side faces a neighbour's blank or high-windowed side (Chapin). In A1, A2 and A6 this means the sibling units' blank walls face each other across the court, and their glass faces outward or down the slope.
2. **Layered privacy.** Shared court or deck, then each home's own porch or deck, then its interior. Give each home a porch of at least 2.4 m deep facing the shared space.
3. **Share the heavy, occasional things:** laundry, guest room, tanks, solar, tool store and parking (Dappled Dwellings, WINC, Gen Y). Sharing also supports the legal "in conjunction with" test (section 4).
4. **Linkable, separable pavilions** (Three House, Kindred's swing room). A pavilion that can be joined to or separated from its neighbour later keeps approval and family options open.
5. **Cars at the edge.** Keep parking at road level or under the rear building, away from the shared court.

**Space-saving moves that stay compliant:**
- **Cavity sliders** can be used, but not over the toilet's 1200 × 900 clear zone. Keep them off walls that need grab-rail noggings (LHDS cl 4.2 note, 6.1(3)).
- **Put the washer and dryer in the bathroom or a hall cupboard.** Designer Eco's Two Bedroom combines bath and laundry, and NSW social-housing studios have a "single bathroom including laundry" (NSW LAHC). Under framings F1 and F3, **one shared laundry** removes the need for a laundry in each sibling unit altogether.
- **Use a 2.1 m kitchen ceiling** under a skillion's low side, and put the living room under the high side.
- **Use one wet wall per building**, with bathroom and kitchenette back to back.
- **Use a bed alcove instead of a bedroom.** A 2.2–2.4 m-deep alcove off the living room, with a curtain or slider, saves about 4 m² against a 3.0 × 3.0 bedroom plus door and corridor.
- **Treat the deck as the second living room.** It is not counted in GFA ([SCPS Schedule 1](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22007624/File/document)), and it is the Queensland outdoor room ([design-precedents.md](design-precedents.md)).

## 4. Rules: how three homes can be approved on this lot

This section adds to [planning-rules.md](planning-rules.md) and does not repeat it. Definitions are quoted from the [Planning Regulation 2017, schedule 24, current as at 11 September 2026](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/pdf/inforce/current/sl-2017-0078).

### 4.1 The definitions that decide it

| Term | Definition (quoted) | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| Dwelling | "all or part of a building that (a) is used, or capable of being used, as a self-contained residence; and (b) contains (i) food preparation facilities; and (ii) a bath or shower; and (iii) a toilet; and (iv) a wash basin; and (v) facilities for washing clothes" | A sibling home that has all five is a dwelling, whatever it is called. One that shares a laundry arguably is not (see [planning-rules.md §2.1](planning-rules.md)). |
| Dwelling house | "(a) 1 dwelling and any domestic outbuildings ...; or (b) 2 dwellings, 1 of which is a secondary dwelling" | A dwelling house can never contain three dwellings. |
| Dual occupancy | "(a) ... 2 dwellings (whether attached or detached) on a single lot ...; but (b) does not include a residential use of premises that involves a secondary dwelling" | **"Dual occupancy + secondary dwelling" is impossible by definition.** Adding a granny flat to a dual occupancy makes three dwellings. |
| Multiple dwelling | "(a) ... a residential use of premises involving 3 or more dwellings, whether attached or detached; but (b) does not include build to rent housing" | Three self-contained homes = a multiple dwelling. It is not listed in the zone's consistent or potentially consistent uses, so it is an **inconsistent use** needing impact assessment ([SCPS 6.2.1, Table 6.2.1.2.1](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22407316/File/document)). |
| Rooming accommodation | Residential accommodation where each resident "(i) has a right to occupy 1 or more rooms ...; (ii) does not have a right to occupy the whole of the premises; (iii) does not occupy a self-contained unit ... or has only limited facilities available for private use; and (iv) shares other rooms, facilities ... with 1 or more other residents". Examples: "boarding house, hostel ..." | Listed as a **potentially consistent** use in Low Density Residential, but it is not in Table 5.5.1, so it is impact assessable ([SCPS Table 5.5.1](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22407271/File/document); [Table 6.2.1.2.1](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22407316/File/document)). It is aimed at boarding houses, and a family is better served by one dwelling house. Not recommended. |
| Household | "1 or more individuals who live together in a dwelling" | A couple and two siblings can be one household in one dwelling house. |
| Domestic outbuilding | "a non-habitable class 10a building that is (a) a shed, garage or carport; and (b) ancillary to a residential use" | A shared carport or laundry shed is an outbuilding. A sleeping pod is **not**: it is habitable. |

**Building code side.**
- Under the NCC a Class 1a building is "one or more buildings, which together form a single dwelling". The NCC explains that a dwelling can include habitable outbuildings such as sleep-outs, and that habitable structures cannot be Class 10 ([NCC 2022 Vol 1, Part A6, A6G2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/a-governing-requirements/part-a6-building-classification)). So **detached pods can legally be parts of one house**.
- External walls less than 1.8 m from another building on the same allotment need fire protection, **except** walls facing "an appurtenant Class 10 building" or "a detached part of the same Class 1 building" ([NCC 2022 Housing Provisions 9.2.1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/housing-provisions/9-fire-safety/part-92-fire-separation-external-walls)).
  - Pods that are all part of **one** dwelling can sit closer than 1.8 m with no fire-rated walls.
  - Pods that are **separate dwellings** (house plus secondary dwelling) need 1.8 m clear, or fire-rated walls. Attached separate dwellings need the FRL 60/60/60 separating wall described in [design-precedents.md](design-precedents.md).

**Case law.** In *Machado v Council of the City of Gold Coast* [2024] QPEC 22 the court found houses with a "partitioned off" second dwelling and "1.8m high fences dividing the backyards" were dual occupancies. The fences "preclude[d] the two dwellings from being used in conjunction with each other" ([Colin Biggers & Paisley summary](https://www.cbp.com.au/insights/publications/dual-occupancies-dressed-up-as-dwellings-houses-planning-and-environment-court-of-queensland-dismis)).
- **Design lesson:** a shared deck, shared court, shared laundry, one entry path, one letterbox and no dividing fences are evidence that the homes are used "in conjunction with" each other. That is the legal test for a secondary dwelling, and it also supports treating wings as parts of one dwelling. The cluster layouts in section 5 help the legal case as well as the social one.

**Tiny houses on wheels** (dropped at the owners' request). Council treats a tiny house on wheels as a vehicle used "for temporary accommodation use only". It needs a local law permit with time limits, and once fixed to land it is a normal Class 1a dwelling ([SCC Tiny houses information sheet, Aug 2024](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/21571411/File/document); [SCC temporary home permit, $622](https://www.sunshinecoast.qld.gov.au/Living-and-Community/Community-Support/temporary-home)). It is not a route to a permanent third home.

## 5. Nine three-home arrangements for the pocket

### How to read these

**The pocket.** Assumed to be 20 m wide at the Foley Road (front, SSW, uphill) edge, tapering evenly on both sides to 13 m at the back (NNE, downhill, sun, highway), 15 m deep.
- That trapezoid is about 248 m². It is deliberately smaller than the owners' ~300 m² and the repo's 341 m² "building zone", so anything that fits here should fit the real shape. Re-check on the site model before fixing anything.
- Coordinates: **x** runs across the pocket from its front-left corner (0–20 m); **d** runs back from the front edge (0–15 m). At depth d the usable width runs from x = 0.233·d to x = 20 − 0.233·d.
- Every footprint below was checked against that taper with a short script.

**Levels.** The front is roughly level with the existing pad. The ground falls 3–4 m toward the back.
- Front buildings (d ≈ 0–7) sit on short posts with near step-free entry.
- Rear buildings stand 2–4 m high, which gives parking or storage underneath.

**Other assumptions.**
- Areas are gross floor area (GFA) to the outside of walls. Decks and breezeways are extra and are not GFA.
- "Couple %" is the couple's share of total GFA. The target is about 50%, but it is a guide, not a rule.
- "Framing" refers to section 4.2 (F1–F4).
- Room layouts follow the standard units in section 3.3.

**Standard units** (section 3.3 gives the room-by-room lists):
- **S30:** a sibling unit of about 4.2 × 7.2 m (30 m²).
- **S35–38:** a sibling unit of about 4.2 × 9.0 m or 4.8 × 7.2 m.

## Rules to confirm

Ask Council for written advice ([development advice services](https://www.sunshinecoast.qld.gov.au/development/development-applications/development-advice-services)), and ask your certifier. Take one chosen arrangement's plans with you.

1. **F1 (one dwelling, three wings).** Would Council treat a house with two sibling wings, each with a bedroom, bathroom and kitchenette, plus one shared laundry, as a single dwelling house? This adds the third wing to [planning-rules.md](planning-rules.md) item 1.
2. **F3 (two siblings in one secondary dwelling).**
   - Is a secondary dwelling with two bedrooms/studios, two kitchenettes and one laundry acceptable as **one** dwelling?
   - Is a 60/60 or 60/70 split "subordinate"? How is the 60 m² GFA measured where the pair shares a breezeway?
   - Can the secondary dwelling be two detached pods forming one dwelling (A1, A6)?
3. **Detached pods as parts of one dwelling.** Will the certifier accept detached habitable pods, with no kitchen or laundry, as "a detached part of the same Class 1 building", so that no fire rating is needed within 1.8 m (NCC 9.2.1)? Does a continuous roof over a breezeway change that?
4. **Freestanding secondary dwelling distance.** Confirm the 20 m limit (AO12.2) is met for every arrangement. They are all within 15 m, but confirm how it is measured.
5. **Livable housing on a slope.** Does the step-free path exemption (H8D2) apply to each home? For the road-edge sibling units a 1:14 path may be feasible, so the exemption would not apply. The internal items apply regardless.
6. **Parking.** Dwelling house: 2 spaces. Secondary dwelling: +1, not in tandem (AO14). Where do three households' cars go? Options are a road-level carport or undercroft bays under the couple's home.
7. **Infrastructure charges and Unitywater** for a secondary dwelling holding two people (as in [planning-rules.md](planning-rules.md) items 3–4).
8. **Owner-builder.** QBCC says owner-builders cannot build "attached granny flats" ([build-costs.md](build-costs.md)). Would a detached F3 sibling pair, or an F1 single dwelling, be eligible?
9. **Pocket shape.** Re-measure the real buildable pocket on the site model. The arrangements assume a symmetric 20 → 13 m taper over 15 m (about 248 m²), which may not match the real boundary, setbacks or easement.
10. **Product class.** For any small product (especially Eastcoast "park cabins" and CabinTech-type expandables), get written confirmation that it is certified as a Class 1a dwelling for permanent occupation, at the BAL and wind rating the site needs.
