## Bottom line

**Most viable path:**
- **Stage 1: one dwelling house.** Build the siblings' building as a single dwelling, not as a house plus an attached secondary dwelling. A dwelling house on this lot is accepted development (no planning DA) if it meets the Dwelling house code and the overlay codes' acceptable outcomes. The key overlay items are a geotechnical certification for the landslide overlay and firefighting water for the bushfire overlay. A building approval from a private certifier is still needed.
- **Stage 2: a secondary dwelling.** This keeps the lot's one secondary dwelling "slot" free for the couple's home.
  - At **60 m² GFA or less**, and meeting the other acceptable outcomes, it needs no planning approval.
  - At **70–90 m²** it misses the 60 m² acceptable outcome. It then goes to Council as a concurrence-agency referral with the building application, where it must show it is "small in size and clearly ancillary". That is a discretionary judgement and the outcome is uncertain.
  - GFA excludes unenclosed balconies and car parking, so decks and an open undercroft do not count.
  - Since 26 September 2022 the secondary dwelling may be rented to anyone.
- **Fallback for Stage 2: a dual occupancy.** This is a code-assessable DA to Council; the biodiversity overlay and the site's slope make it so. Code assessment must be approved if the benchmarks are met or can be met by conditions. The hurdles are:
  - the reticulated sewer requirement (the lot appears to sit outside Unitywater's mapped connection areas);
  - private open space on slopes of 5% or less;
  - 2 parking spaces per dwelling;
  - roughly $22k–$38k extra in infrastructure charges.

**What to avoid:**
- **Two full dwellings in Stage 1.** If each half has a kitchen, bathroom, toilet, basin *and* its own laundry, the building is two dwellings. It is then either a dwelling house plus secondary dwelling (the secondary part capped at 60 m² for accepted development) or a dual occupancy. Either way the lot's two-dwelling limit is used up, and a Stage 2 building would be a third dwelling: a "multiple dwelling", which is not a consistent use in the zone and needs impact assessment.
- **Assuming a "kitchenette" label decides it.** No primary source says so. The legal test is whether the part is "capable of being used as a self-contained residence" with all five listed facilities. Sharing one laundry is the clearest design feature that keeps Stage 1 a single dwelling. Get Council's written view on the plan before lodging.

**State koala rules do not stop building in the existing clearing.**
- The state koala prohibition bites only on "interfering with koala habitat", which means removing or destroying native vegetation. Building in the existing clearing with no clearing does not engage it (section 3.5).
- If a DA is ever lodged (e.g. for a dual occupancy), the koala assessment benchmarks drop away where the "development footprint" is 500 m² or less.
- Separately, whether the existing clearing happened after 7 February 2020 matters for the 500 m² clearing exemption if you ever clear anything.

**Services are the sleeper issue.**
- On Unitywater's public mapping the lot sits outside the water and sewer connection areas, although mains run past it.
- If you cannot connect, the Dwelling house code requires on-site effluent treatment and a 45,000 L rainwater tank for household use. The bushfire overlay adds a 5,000 L firefighting reserve.
- The dual occupancy route would fail its sewer acceptable outcome.
- A Unitywater Service Advice Notice should be the first step.

**Siting rules.** Side setbacks and site cover still come from the 2010 QDC MP1.2: 1.5 m side/rear clearance up to 4.5 m high, 2 m up to 7.5 m, and 50% site cover. The new Queensland Housing Code (from 1 September 2026) applies on the Sunshine Coast only once Council amends its scheme, and Council has not done so.

## 1. Definitions and levels of assessment in the Low Density Residential Zone

### 1.1 Definitions

The Planning Regulation definitions apply, and the SCPS repeats them.

- **Dwelling** means all or part of a building that "(a) is used, or capable of being used, as a self-contained residence; and (b) contains— (i) food preparation facilities; and (ii) a bath or shower; and (iii) a toilet; and (iv) a wash basin; and (v) facilities for washing clothes" ([Planning Regulation 2017 sch 24](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/sl-2017-0078)). The SCPS definition is to the same effect ([SCPS Schedule 1, SC1.2](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22007624/File/document)).
- **Dwelling house** means "a residential use of premises involving— (a) 1 dwelling and any domestic outbuildings associated with the dwelling; or (b) 2 dwellings, 1 of which is a secondary dwelling, and any domestic outbuildings associated with either dwelling" (Planning Regulation sch 24, amended by 2022 SL No. 133).
- **Secondary dwelling** means "a dwelling on a lot that is used in conjunction with, but subordinate to, another dwelling on the lot, whether or not the dwelling is— (a) attached to the other dwelling; or (b) occupied by individuals who are related to, or associated with, the household of the other dwelling" (Planning Regulation sch 24, substituted by 2022 SL No. 133). The SCPS adds: "A secondary dwelling may be constructed under a dwelling house, be attached to a dwelling house or be freestanding" ([SCPS Schedule 1](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22007624/File/document)).
- **Dual occupancy** "(a) means a residential use of premises involving— (i) 2 dwellings (whether attached or detached) on a single lot ...; but (b) does not include a residential use of premises that involves a secondary dwelling" (Planning Regulation sch 24).
- **Household** means "1 or more individuals who live together in a dwelling" (Planning Regulation sch 24, substituted by 2022 SL No. 133).
- **Multiple dwelling**: "Premises that contain three or more dwellings" ([SCPS Schedule 1](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22007624/File/document)). It is not a consistent or potentially consistent use in the zone, and "a use not listed in Table 6.2.1.2.1 is an inconsistent use and is not intended to occur in the Low density residential zone" ([SCPS 6.2.1 Low density residential zone code, Table 6.2.1.2.1](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22407316/File/document)).
- **Gross floor area** is measured to the outside of external walls. It excludes, among other things, "(e) the parking, loading and manoeuvring of motor vehicles; (f) unenclosed private balconies whether roofed or not" ([SCPS Schedule 1](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22007624/File/document)).
- **Basement** means a space between floor levels, "(b) no part of which is more than 1m above ground level" (Planning Regulation sch 24). This matters because the Schedule 6 exemption in section 3.1 does not apply if the development involves "a basement or an underground parking area". An open undercroft on a slope that stands more than 1 m above ground is not a basement.

## 2. What Stage 1 and Stage 2 could be approved as

### 2.1 The "two kitchens" question

- No primary source found from Council or the State uses "second kitchen", "kitchenette" or "wet bar" as a test. Council's guides and the scheme rely only on the definition of **dwelling**: a part of a building "used, or capable of being used, as a self-contained residence" that contains all of food preparation facilities, a bath or shower, a toilet, a wash basin and "facilities for washing clothes" (Planning Regulation sch 24).
- **Interpretation, not confirmed by Council.** If one half has all five facilities, it is capable of being a second dwelling whatever the room is called. If it lacks one of them, it arguably is not a separate dwelling, and the building is one dwelling. The most defensible example is a single shared laundry reached from both halves.
  - Council's checklist looks at function: "nexus", "joint residential endeavour" and "shared services".
  - Because the definition says "capable of being used", an assessor may look past labels to the plumbing and layout.
- Get a written view from Council before relying on this (see "Unconfirmed / ask council").
- **Building code consequence.** Under NCC A6G2(2)(a), Class 1a is "one or more buildings, which together form a single dwelling including ... A detached house" or "One of a group of two or more attached dwellings, each being a building, separated by a fire-resisting wall" ([NCC 2022 Vol 1, Part A6](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/a-governing-requirements/part-a6-building-classification)). Two attached dwellings under one roof therefore need fire-resisting separation (and, per Council's secondary dwelling sheet, sound transmission checks). A single dwelling does not. The certifier decides.

### 2.3 Stage 2 options (assuming Stage 1 is Option A)

**Secondary dwelling of 60 m² GFA or less:**
- Accepted development if it meets AO11–AO14 and the overlay acceptable outcomes. If freestanding, it must be within 20 m of the house.
- 60 m² GFA for 2 bed + bath + powder + kitchen + living is tight. Unenclosed decks and car parking are excluded from GFA.

**Secondary dwelling of 70–90 m²:**
- It fails AO12.1(b), the 60 m² cap.
- **If** all "relevant overlays" (section 3.1) are met at acceptable-outcome level, Planning Regulation sch 6 s 2(2) prevents the material change of use being assessable. Instead the building application is referred to Council as a concurrence agency under sch 9 pt 3 div 2 table 8. Council assesses it against "the relevant provisions of a local instrument that would apply ... if schedule 6, part 2, section 2(2) did not apply", i.e. PO12: "small in size and clearly ancillary to the primary dwelling" ([Planning Regulation sch 9 pt 3 div 2 table 8](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/sl-2017-0078); [Dwelling house guide s 10.0](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/21571398/File/document)).
- If a relevant overlay's AO is not met, it becomes a code-assessable MCU instead.
- Either way the test is discretionary. A 70–90 m² second home beside a 100–120 m² main house is proportionally large, and Council's checklist warns that a secondary dwelling of a scale that "constitutes a material change of use for a Dual Occupancy" is not a secondary dwelling.

**Dual occupancy:**
- A code-assessable DA: the biodiversity overlay makes a dual occupancy MCU code assessable (section 3.2), and the lot fails AO1.2(c) on slope.
- Code assessment means no public notification. Under the Planning Act s 60(2) the assessment manager "must decide to approve the application to the extent the development complies with all of the assessment benchmarks" and may refuse non-compliance "only if compliance can not be achieved by imposing development conditions" ([Planning Act 2016 s 60](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2016-025)).
- Likely issues:
  - AO10 requires a sewer connection (see section 5 on connection areas);
  - AO4 requires 25 m² of private open space at a gradient of 5% or less per dwelling;
  - AO8 requires 4 car spaces in total;
  - AO3.4 sets side/rear setbacks per QDC MP1.3.
- It also attracts a second-dwelling infrastructure charge (section 5).

**Only one secondary dwelling.** The definition allows "2 dwellings, 1 of which is a secondary dwelling". Council's tiny house sheet confirms that "More than one tiny house on a property may constitute a dual occupancy, multiple dwelling or relocatable home park" ([Tiny houses information sheet, Aug 2024](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/21571411/File/document)).

## 3. How the overlays change the level of assessment

### 3.1 The state override: Planning Regulation sch 6 s 2(2)

A local planning scheme cannot make a material change of use for a dwelling house (which includes a secondary dwelling) assessable if:
- "(a) the material change of use is for a residential purpose in a residential zone other than a medium density residential zone or high density residential zone; and
- (b) the material change of use does not involve a basement or an underground parking area; and
- (c) 1 of the following applies— (i) no relevant overlay applies to the premises; (ii) only an overlay about bush fire hazards applies to the premises and the premises are less than 2,000m2; (iii) a relevant overlay applies to the premises and the application of the overlay does not result in the material change of use being categorised as assessable development."

([Planning Regulation 2017 sch 6 pt 2 s 2(2)](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/sl-2017-0078); amended 2 December 2022 per the [Dwelling house guide s 4.1](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/21571398/File/document).)

**Relevant overlay** means an overlay about "bush fire hazards, coastal hazards, flood hazards or landslide hazards", or one with a code about "the protection of areas of natural, environmental or ecological significance, including ... biodiversity ... wetlands and waterways" (Planning Regulation sch 24, inserted by 2025 SL No. 149).

**For this lot:**
- The relevant overlays are Bushfire, Landslide/steep land, Biodiversity/waterways/wetlands and Flood hazard.
- The Scenic amenity, Regional infrastructure, Airport environs (OLS) and Height overlays are not relevant overlays.
- The lot is over 2,000 m² and has several overlays, so only limb (c)(iii) can apply.
- **If the house meets every acceptable outcome of those four overlay codes**, the material change of use cannot be assessable. Any shortfall against the Dwelling house code (e.g. a secondary dwelling over 60 m², or height over 8.5 m) then goes to Council as a concurrence referral on the building application instead (sch 9 pt 3 div 2 table 8).
- **If an overlay acceptable outcome is not met**, the overlay makes the MCU code assessable under SCPS s 5.3.3(2) and a DA is needed.

### 3.2 Each overlay on this lot

Sources: [SCPS Table 5.10.1 Overlays](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22407314/File/document) and the overlay codes in [Part 8](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22007627/File/document). In Table 5.10.1, "No change" means the category stays as in Table 5.5.1 (accepted) but the overlay code's acceptable outcomes become requirements. Failing them triggers code assessment (s 5.3.3(2)).

**Landslide hazard and steep land** ([code 8.2.10](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22407380/File/document))
- Effect on a dwelling house MCU: "No change" for any use other than animal husbandry or cropping.

### 3.5 State koala, vegetation, state-controlled road and building-code provisions

All from the [Planning Regulation 2017, current reprint](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/sl-2017-0078) unless another source is linked.

**Koala: prohibition and assessment (sch 10 pt 10)**
- Part 10 "applies in relation to development on a lot that is completely or partly in the SEQ region" (s 16).
- **s 16A(1):** "Development is prohibited development to the extent the development involves interfering with koala habitat in an area that is both—(a) a koala priority area; and (b) a koala habitat area." The lot is mapped as both.
- **"Interfering with koala habitat"** (sch 24) "(a) means removing, cutting down, ringbarking, pushing over, poisoning or destroying in any way, including by burning, flooding or draining, native vegetation in a koala habitat area; but (b) does not include destroying standing vegetation by stock, or lopping a tree".
  - "Native vegetation" excludes grass and non-woody herbage ([Vegetation Management Act 1999 s 8](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-1999-090)).
  - So building in the existing clearing, without removing native trees or shrubs, is not "interfering". The s 16A prohibition and the s 16B state assessment are not engaged.
- **Exemption relevant if any clearing is ever proposed** ("exempted development", sch 24 para (k)): "development ... that results in a total area on the premises of 500m2 or less of 1 or more koala habitat areas being cleared of native vegetation since 7 February 2020". If the existing ~520 m² clearing was made after 7 February 2020, it counts toward that 500 m². The clearing date is not confirmed.
- **Koala benchmarks when a DA is made and no habitat is interfered with** (sch 10 pt 10 div 5; sch 11 pt 2):
  - The category of assessment is "the category of assessment stated for the development in a local categorising instrument". The state does not elevate it.
  - For a DA to which sch 11 pt 2 applies, the benchmarks are "safe koala movement measures" (sch 11 s 4(1)(a)), and either every building or work is "at least 50m from a koala habitat area" or the s 4(2) criteria are met. Those criteria include that a building is "located to minimise the amount of vegetation required to be cleared for safety purposes".
  - **But** the schedule "does not apply in relation to a development application for development if ... (g) the development results in a development footprint of 500m2 or less" (sch 11 s 1(g)). "Development footprint" is "the total area of the premises covered by a building, structure or landscaping" (sch 24).
  - A house plus a later small dwelling is likely to be under 500 m², depending on how driveways and landscaping are counted (not confirmed).
- **Clearing, if it ever happens:** the [Nature Conservation (Koala) Conservation Plan 2017](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2017-0152) s 11 requires clearing of koala habitat trees with a trunk over 10 cm at 1.3 m to be "carried out in the presence of a koala spotter". Section 10 sets sequential clearing conditions.

**Vegetation management (Category B remnant)**
- Clearing native vegetation on prescribed land is assessable operational work "unless the clearing is—(a) exempt clearing work; or (b) accepted development" (sch 10 pt 3 s 5).
- Exemptions (sch 21) include "residential clearing", which is tied to building work under a development permit, and some urban-purpose clearing of least-concern REs. These vegetation exemptions do **not** switch off the koala prohibition.
- No clearing is proposed, so none of this is triggered.
- Separately, the SCPS Biodiversity overlay AO1.3 bars a dwelling house on an endangered or of-concern RE "unless its location is in accordance with a Property Map of Assessable Vegetation" (section 3.2). The RE status of the Category B mapping is not confirmed.

**State-controlled road (Nambour Connection Road)**
- **MCU referral to SARA** (sch 10 pt 9 div 4 subdiv 2 table 4) applies to an MCU "other than an excluded material change of use, that is assessable development under a local categorising instrument", if the premises are within 25 m of a State transport corridor, or adjacent to a road that intersects a State-controlled road and within 100 m of the intersection.
- **"Excluded material change of use"** (sch 24) covers an MCU that "(a) does not involve a new or changed access between the premises and any of the following—(i) a State transport corridor; (ii) a road that intersects with a State-controlled road ...; and (b) is for— ... (A) a dwelling house; (B) a secondary dwelling; ... (E) a dual occupancy".
- **Result:**
  - A house, secondary dwelling or dual occupancy using the existing Foley Road access is excluded, so there is no state referral.
  - If a DA is needed (e.g. dual occupancy) **and** it creates a new or changed access onto Foley Road, and Foley Road intersects Nambour Connection Road within 100 m, it could need SARA referral against SDAP State code 1.
  - Keep the existing access point.
- **Building work** referral near a State transport corridor (sch 9 pt 3 div 1 table 3) is only for a "non-residential purpose", so it is not relevant.
- **Road works or new access to the state road itself:** [Transport Infrastructure Act 1994](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-1994-008) s 33 requires the chief executive's written approval for road works on, or interference with, a State-controlled road. Not engaged if access stays on Foley Road.
- **Transport noise:** QDC MP4.4 applies to a class 1 building "located in a transport noise corridor". Such a corridor must be designated under Chapter 8B of the [Building Act 1975](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-1975-011), which is possible only within 100 m of a State-controlled road (s 246Z(2)) ([MP4.4, archived copy](https://www.housing.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0015/4830/qdcmp4.4buildingsinatransportnoisecorridor.pdf)). Whether this lot is designated is not confirmed; the certifier will check the state noise corridor mapping.

**Bushfire construction**

## 4. Setbacks, site cover, height and fences for a dwelling house

**Scheme alternative provisions.** The [Dwelling house code](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/22407391/File/document) sets only these "alternative provisions to QDC" for a residential zone:
- **Road frontage setback**, AO3: the dwelling house (other than a garage, carport or shed) "is setback to any road frontage at least:- (a) 4.5 metres for the ground storey; and (b) 6 metres for any levels above the ground storey."
  - "Any road frontage" means both Foley Road and Nambour Connection Road.
- **Garage, carport, shed**, AO2.1: "(a) is setback at least 6 metres from any road frontage; (b) does not exceed a height of 3.6 metres; and (c) has a total floor area that does not exceed 56m²". AO2.1(b) and (c) "do not apply to a garage under the main roof of a dwelling house". An undercroft carport under the house is therefore only subject to the 6 m road setback.
- AO2.2: garage doors facing a street no more than 6 m wide per plane.
- **Height**, AO1: does not exceed the Height of Buildings and Structures Overlay Map height, here **8.5 m**.

**Side/rear setbacks and site cover.**

## 6. Useful Sunshine Coast guidance

All linked from Council's [Help with the planning scheme](https://www.sunshinecoast.qld.gov.au/development/planning-documents/sunshine-coast-planning-scheme-2014/planning-scheme-help) page:

- [Secondary dwelling information sheet (Sept 2023)](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/21571402/File/document). Covers the 60 m² cap, 20 m distance, shared services, renting to anyone since 26 Sept 2022, concurrence referrals, and the Table 1 checklist.
- [Dwelling house guide (May 2024)](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/21571398/File/document). Explains how sch 6 s 2(2) interacts with the overlays, gives a flowchart, and lists the concurrence referrals and QDC alternative provisions. It is the most useful single document here.
- [Dwelling house information sheet](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/21571400/File/document) and [Dual occupancy information sheet (March 2023)](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/21571396/File/document).
- [Tiny houses information sheet (Aug 2024)](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/21571411/File/document):
  - A tiny house fixed to the land is "subject to the same planning, building and plumbing laws as a normal dwelling house". Added to an existing house, it is a secondary dwelling. It is a class 1a building under the BCA.
  - A tiny house on wheels used for temporary accommodation is regulated under "subordinate local law no. 1 (administration) 2016". It needs a local law permit with time limits, except for occupation under 2 weeks in 52 on a vacant property, or under 4 weeks in 52 behind an existing dwelling, with no waste left on site.
  - "More than one tiny house on a property may constitute a dual occupancy, multiple dwelling or relocatable home park".
- [Vegetation management information sheet](https://publicdocs.scc.qld.gov.au/hpecmwebdrawer/Record/23489317/File/document), relevant if any clearing is ever proposed.
- [Council: Flood information relevant to building works](https://www.sunshinecoast.qld.gov.au/development/building/flooding-and-building), covering the flood hazard area for building regulation purposes and flood searches.
- For written advice, Council offers a fee-based service ([Development advice services](https://www.sunshinecoast.qld.gov.au/development/development-applications/development-advice-services)).

**State guidance:**
- State planning department, "Changes to secondary dwellings" ([page](https://www.planning.qld.gov.au/planning-issues-and-interests/changes-to-secondary-dwellings); the live page blocks automated access, so it was read from an archived copy updated 16 Sep 2025):
  - "Any new secondary dwelling can now be rented to anyone".
  - "you will still need to speak with your council to understand if you need development approval".
  - "Any new secondary dwelling will still require building approval".
  - A rented granny flat may attract "additional fire and sound transmission requirements of the building code".
- [QBCC tiny homes FAQ](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/home-owner-hub/build-renovate/getting-started/options-building-renovating/faqs-tiny-homes) (updated 7 May 2026):
  - "Tiny homes are subject to the same planning, building and plumbing laws that apply to traditional homes."
  - "A tiny home on a trailer with wheels that is not fixed to land or connected to utilities is treated as a caravan ... Once fixed to land and used as a dwelling, it becomes a Class 1a building and must meet all building, planning and plumbing requirements."
  - No 2025–26 state law was found that allows living in caravans or tiny homes on residential lots outside these rules.

## Unconfirmed / ask council

1. **Stage 1 as one dwelling.** Would Council treat a building with two living wings, one full kitchen, a kitchenette/wet bar in the second wing, two bathrooms and **one shared laundry** as a single dwelling (not a dwelling house plus secondary dwelling)? No primary source addresses "second kitchens". Ask for written advice through Council's [development advice service](https://www.sunshinecoast.qld.gov.au/development/development-applications/development-advice-services) with a floor plan.
2. **Stage 2 at 70–90 m².** Would Council, as concurrence agency, accept a secondary dwelling of 70–90 m² GFA as "small in size and clearly ancillary" (PO12) beside a 100–120 m² main house? Or would it treat it as a dual occupancy? Ask the same written-advice request.
3. **Water and sewer.** Can the lot connect to Unitywater water and sewer? Our reading of Unitywater's 2024 GIS puts the lot outside the connection areas, though mains are adjacent and a sewer pressure main crosses it. Get a Unitywater Service Advice Notice. The answer decides Dwelling house code AO7.1–AO7.3 (45,000 L tank / on-site effluent) and the dual occupancy's AO10.
4. **Infrastructure charges on a secondary dwelling.** Does Council's Resolution No. 9 or Unitywater's schedule levy anything for a secondary dwelling? Neither document mentions secondary dwellings. What are the current indexed amounts (base March 2021) for a second dwelling and for the vacant-lot credit?
5. **Mapping details** to confirm on MyMaps / the Development.i site report:
   - that the lot is not in Precinct LDR1;
   - the extent of the Flood hazard overlay "flooding and inundation area" versus the separate "flood hazard area for building regulation purposes" buffer and overland flow path;
   - the waterway high-bank location for the 10 m buffer (Biodiversity AO1.2);
   - the regional ecosystem status of the Category B vegetation (AO1.3);
   - whether the lot is in a designated transport noise corridor (QDC MP4.4).
6. **Landslide.** Confirm that a geotechnical certification by a "competent person" (landslide overlay AO1/AO2) keeps the house accepted development. Confirm what Council expects in it (Planning scheme policy for the landslide hazard and steep land overlay code).
7. **Drainage easement A.** What are the easement's terms, and will Council consent to any structure, driveway or fill over or near it?
8. **Koala.** When was the existing ~520 m² clearing made (before or after 7 February 2020)? This matters only if any clearing is ever proposed (exempted development para (k)). Does a driveway or landscaping count toward the 500 m² "development footprint" in sch 11 s 1(g)? Does sch 11 pt 2 apply at all to a building application assessed by a private certifier (it refers to a "development application")?
9. **State road access.** Does Foley Road intersect Nambour Connection Road within 100 m of the lot? This matters only if a DA is lodged **and** the access point changes.
10. **Queensland Housing Code.** Has Council resolved or started an amendment to adopt the QHC before 1 September 2029? If so, the side setback and site cover numbers may change. The QHC text itself could not be retrieved.
11. **"Subordinate" with near-equal halves.** If Stage 1 were done as house plus attached secondary dwelling (not recommended), would Council accept, for example, a 60/60 split, or require the secondary part to be clearly smaller?
12. **Height on a slope.** How does Council measure 8.5 m for a high-set house over an open undercroft on 15–25% slopes: from natural ground at every point, per the SCPS definition? Is an open undercroft (more than 1 m above ground) treated as not a "basement" for sch 6 s 2(2)(b)?
13. **2022 commencement date.** Council and the state department say 26 September 2022; the Regulation (2022 SL No. 133) was notified 23 September 2022. This is immaterial for new work.
